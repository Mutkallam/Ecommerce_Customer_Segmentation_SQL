# E-Commerce Customer Segmentation & Revenue Analysis
## Tools: PostgreSQL, SQL, pgAdmin, CTEs, Window Functions, Business Analytics

In this project, I analyzed customer behavior, revenue performance, and purchasing patterns using a synthetic e-commerce dataset containing 5,000 orders from 989 customers across multiple product categories and regions.

## Key Steps:
Dataset Preparation: Loaded the e-commerce transaction dataset into PostgreSQL, defined the table schema, and validated the data by checking row counts, date ranges, and missing values across important fields. The dataset contained 5,000 complete records spanning 2022–2035.

### Business Performance Analysis:

Calculated overall business metrics including total orders, unique customers, total revenue, and average order value.

Analyzed product category and regional performance to identify the strongest revenue contributors and compare customer activity across different parts of the business.

### Customer Behavior & Segmentation:

Analyzed customer purchase frequency, lifetime spending, and average order value to identify the highest-value customers and compare repeat buyers with one-time customers.

Used CTEs and CASE statements to segment customers into High Value, Loyal, Regular, and Low Frequency groups based on order frequency and total spending.

### Advanced SQL Analysis:

Used the LAG() window function to calculate month-over-month revenue changes and examine how sales varied over time.

Applied cumulative SUM() window functions to measure customer revenue concentration and determine how much of total revenue was generated by the highest-spending customers.

Analyzed discount levels, delivery times, customer ratings, and order revenue to evaluate additional factors associated with purchasing behavior and business performance.

### Key Takeaways:

The dataset contained 5,000 orders from 989 unique customers, generating approximately $5.11 million in total revenue with an average order value of about $1,021.96.

Customer activity was strongly repeat-driven, with 950 of 989 customers placing multiple orders and only 39 customers making a single purchase.

Customer segmentation identified 94 High Value customers who averaged approximately $10,589 in total spending and 9.33 orders, compared with Low Frequency customers who averaged about $1,691 in spending and 1.70 orders.

Revenue was relatively distributed across the customer base rather than concentrated among a very small group, with approximately 58% of customers required to generate 80% of total revenue.

The West region generated the highest total revenue at approximately $1.35 million, although revenue remained fairly balanced across the North, South, East, and West regions.

Higher discount levels were generally associated with lower average order revenue, while average quantity purchased remained relatively stable across discount levels.

Delivery time showed little relationship with customer ratings or average order revenue, suggesting that delivery speed was not a major driver of customer satisfaction within this dataset.
